Two eclipses in August, and a wave of forwarded warnings with them.

A solar eclipse on the 12th and a lunar eclipse on the 28th. Here is what each one actually is, and what it means for anyone observing rituals in India.

We are not going to resolve this for you with a number. A lunar eclipse can only be seen where the Moon is above the horizon when it occurs. Sources conflict on India, so this article treats visibility as unconfirmed and asks you to check Drik Panchang for your city.

This eclipse falls on the same day as Raksha Bandhan. If it is not visible from your location, there is no Sutak Kaal and no change to the timing of the ritual. Confirm against your local panchang, since visibility is what decides it.

+The rule that actually decides everything
THE ONE THING WORTH KNOWING
The religious effect of an eclipse applies where the eclipse is visible. Not everywhere on Earth at once.

An eclipse that is total and dramatic over Spain, or partial over the Americas, has no Sutak relevance in India if it cannot be seen from India.

This single fact is what forwarded eclipse warnings almost always leave out, and it is the most useful thing on this page.

+Myths & Facts
"An eclipse affects everyone in India the same way, whether or not it can be seen here."CORRECTION

Visibility determines applicability. This is the consistent position across panchang authorities: where an eclipse cannot be seen, Sutak Kaal and the associated restrictions are not observed. Messages that treat every eclipse worldwide as equally significant everywhere are not reflecting the rule.

+What the Panchang measures, and what people add to it
THE PANCHANG GIVES YOU
Two things, both computed

When the eclipse occurs, and where it is visible. That is the whole of it.

EVERYTHING ELSE
Layered on by practice

What to do during Sutak, whether to eat, whether to bathe afterwards, whether to keep an idol covered.

Those practices are kept in many households with genuine sincerity, and they vary by family and region. They are not what the Panchang says. The Panchang gives you timing and visibility; what you do with that is your tradition's to decide, not ours to prescribe.

+What this means for your plans

If you have a ritual, a vrat or a booking around either date, the only thing that changes anything for you in India is whether the eclipse is actually visible from your city.

The 12 August Surya Grahan is not visible from India. The 28 August Chandra Grahan's India visibility is unsettled across sources and is worth a final check closer to the date.

There is no need to move Raksha Bandhan, Sawan observances or anything else on the basis of eclipse anxiety alone. Check your local panchang, and proceed normally unless it says otherwise for your location.
