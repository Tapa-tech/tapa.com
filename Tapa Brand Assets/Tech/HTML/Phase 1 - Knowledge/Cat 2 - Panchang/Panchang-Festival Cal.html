No classification tag and no Confidence Score. This is timing data, not a ritual-authority claim. Regenerates annually.

Ghatasthapana falls on Sunday 11 October 2026.

Pratipada begins on the evening of 10 October, at approximately 9:19 PM, and ends at approximately 9:30 PM on 11 October. Ghatasthapana is performed on the morning of the 11th while Pratipada is prevailing and before Hindu midday. This is the sunrise-based calculation, which is what governs.

The 12 October figure circulating on some sites comes from a different method of resolving Pratipada. It is the single most common Navratri confusion each year.

TWO WINDOWS, AND ONE CUT-OFF
Morning — preferred 6:19 – 10:12 AM The first third of the day, while Pratipada prevails.

After Hindu midday Not performed Ghatasthapana is not done after midday.
+Two things that make 2026 unusual
ONE
Saptami is long

It covers both 17 and 18 October, so two civil days carry the same tithi label and the observance stretches across ten days rather than nine. This is normal and nothing has gone wrong.

TWO
Ashtami and Navami merge

On 19 October. Ashtami ends at approximately 10:51 AM and Navami begins at that same moment, with the Sandhi Puja window falling across the join.

+Where panchangs disagree
STATED, NOT HIDDEN
Because of that merge, panchangs differ on when Durga Ashtami is observed.
DRIK PANCHANG

Lists Durga Ashtami and Maha Navami together on 19 October.

OTHERS, INCLUDING PROKERALA

Place Ashtami with Mahagauri on 18 October and Navami with Siddhidatri on 19 October.

Both are defensible. This is a difference of panchang convention, not of scripture, and we will not present one as the correct answer.

Follow your family or community panchang. If you have none, follow the one your local temple uses.

+Vijayadashami

Tuesday 20 October 2026. The tenth day, and not part of the nine-night worship arc — it marks the outcome.
