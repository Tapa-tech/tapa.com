Ramcharitmanas: The Seven Kandas Explained

What each section contains, why each matters, and where Sundarkand fits.

📖No prior reading needed 🕉No Sanskrit required ⏱6 minutes to read ✓Start anywhere you like
JUMP TO 📚 The seven kandas Why Sundarkand Where to start 💭 Common worries

The map before the journey.

The Ramcharitmanas is Tulsidas's retelling of the Ramayana in Awadhi, and it is divided into seven sections called kandas. Each covers a phase of Ram's story.

If you are starting with Sundarkand — as most people do — this shows you where it sits and what surrounds it.

+The seven kandas

In order. Sundarkand is the fifth.

1
Bala Kanda बालकाण्ड

Ram's birth and childhood, the breaking of Shiva's bow, the marriage to Sita.

2
Ayodhya Kanda अयोध्याकाण्ड

The exile. Dasharatha's promise, Kaikeyi's demand, the departure for the forest, Bharat's refusal of the throne.

3
Aranya Kanda अरण्यकाण्ड

The forest years. Shurpanakha, the golden deer, the abduction of Sita, Jatayu.

4
Kishkindha Kanda किष्किन्धाकाण्ड

The alliance with the vanaras. Sugriva, Vali, and the decision to send Hanuman south.

5
Sundarkand सुन्दरकाण्ड MOST RECITED

Hanuman alone. The leap to Lanka, the search, finding Sita, the burning of Lanka, the return.

6
Lanka Kanda लंकाकाण्ड

The war. The bridge, the battle, Ravana, the return to Ayodhya.

7
Uttar Kanda उत्तरकाण्ड

After the return. Ram's reign, and Tulsidas's closing teachings.

+Why Sundarkand is the one people recite

Every other kanda is about Ram. Sundarkand is about a devotee — sent on an errand nobody was sure could be completed, with no army, no certainty and no way back if it went wrong.

It is also the only kanda that ends entirely in success. Nothing is lost in it.

The exile is not reversed, the war has not started, and for the length of one section a difficult thing is attempted and simply works. That is a reasonable thing to want to read aloud on a Tuesday evening.

+Where to start

You do not need to read the first four kandas before Sundarkand. Most households that recite it weekly have never read the Uttar Kanda at all.

If you want the story in order, start at Bala Kanda and read at your own pace. If you want to begin the practice, start at Sundarkand.

+Common worries — answered

Every one of these has been asked by someone opening the book for the first time.

?"Should I read the whole thing first?"
→
No. Starting at Sundarkand is the normal way in, and it is how most households begin.
?"Is the Ramcharitmanas the same as the Ramayana?"
→
It is a retelling. Valmiki's Ramayana is the older Sanskrit epic; Tulsidas wrote the Ramcharitmanas in Awadhi in the sixteenth century so that people without Sanskrit could read it. Both are read; they are different books.
?"Which edition should I buy?"
→
The Gita Press Gorakhpur edition is the standard one and is inexpensive. Any edition with a transliteration you can read is fine.
?"My Hindi is not strong enough for Awadhi."
→
Almost nobody's is. Every common edition prints a Hindi paraphrase alongside the verse, and many print a transliteration too. Read the verse aloud and the meaning beside it.

Seven books, one story, and no obligation to read them in order. The households that recite Sundarkand every Tuesday are not skipping ahead — they are doing the thing the tradition has done for four centuries.

Find out where Sundarkand sits. Then open it, and start.
